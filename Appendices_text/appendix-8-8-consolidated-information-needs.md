## Contextual and enabling information needs

These needs are not tied to any single decision. They underpin the needs above by making decisions possible, legitimate, understood and funded, and are grouped by the function they enable rather than by surveillance layer.

### Governance, authority, and coordination

*Information that enables decisions to exist, be legitimate, and be coordinated.*

- Mapping of legal and regulatory frameworks and governance structures relevant to epidemic response, including real-time data availability and sharing
- Mapping of key decision-makers by sectors and levels and their specific data requirements across levels
- Documented definitions of epidemic or crisis phases and the associated rules that determine when decisions and measures are escalated or de-escalated.
- Governance arrangements for declaring, maintaining, and ending a state of emergency
- Accountability mechanisms linked to preparedness and response decisions
- Information on the national political landscape and ethical values

### Communication, interpretation, and coordination

*Information that enables shared understanding and aligned action.*

- Reliability, adequacy, and preferred formats of communication systems
- Listing of barriers to understanding or using data by different levels of decision-makers, including contextual interpretation
- Past communication breakdowns or delays and lessons learned
- Presence and mapping of science-to-policy translators
- Identification of single points of contact for crisis coordination and policy dissemination (local level, primary care, hospital, health authority levels)

### Financing, sustainability, and learning

*Information that allows preparedness and response to be funded, sustained, and improved.*

- Availability of emergency and contingency funding
- Criteria for allocation of additional funding
- Capacity of regions to absorb and use resources effectively
- Sustainability of financing models for surveillance and preparedness
- Visibility on long-term financing for preparedness capacities
- Flexibility of funding mechanisms across epidemic phases
- Capacity to rapidly initiate and coordinate research during a crisis
- Integration of research outputs into decision-making processes
- Preparedness to evaluate effectiveness of measures during implementation
